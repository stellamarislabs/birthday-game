## Title

Maria and the Case of the Missing Heart

## Subtitle

Sprawa Zaginionego Serca

## Case File

Case No. 16/05 -- The City v. The Missing Heart

## Story Summary

On the morning of her birthday, Maria arrives at the kancelaria before Warsaw has fully woken up. On her desk is a sealed envelope with no client name, only Case No. 16/05 and the title The City v. The Missing Heart. Inside are a small brass key, a folded tram ticket, and a message asking her to follow a trail of clues across Warsaw, follow the evidence carefully, and not trust the loudest answer.

The envelope leads to the tram route. The tram route leads to the golden stamp. The stamp reveals an ordered path across the city. The brass key opens a hidden wall in a rebuilt street. Behind the wall is a broken image that Maria repairs, revealing the Vistula mark. At the river, a nervous witness gives her a note and runs away. The note says the heart was not stolen, but left willingly.

The witness note points Maria to the archive, where she finds a marginal correction: "No. Given." Hidden in the file spine is a silver key. The key opens the Trust door in the Courthouse of Echoes after Maria asks the right question: "What remains when things are difficult?" Behind the door is a lantern, leading her to a quiet garden. The lantern reveals a soft path and a blue ribbon tied around argument pages.

The blue ribbon leads Maria up the Tower of Arguments, where she learns the strongest argument is not spoken once, but lived. At the top, she finds an unfinished letter. The letter's missing meaning is scattered across the rooftops. There, Maria connects all previous clues: attention, responsibility, patience, truth, details, trust, warmth, promise. The letter completes: "The heart was never stolen. It was given."

The final door opens to the Court of the Heart. Maria places the final meanings into the court seal: Attention, Responsibility, Patience, Truth, Details, Trust, Warmth, Promise, Future, Love. The case file reveals the charge: Maria is accused of stealing the heart. The Secret Client is Alper. But the evidence proves the heart was freely given.

## Story Design Principles

- Every collectible is a clue that leads to the next clue.
- Each puzzle should explain, unlock, repair, inspect, or assemble the clue.
- Maria should feel competent, observant, calm, and respected.
- The romance should be earned by the investigation, not suddenly declared.
- The mystery should gradually shift from "Who stole the heart?" to "Was it ever stolen?"
- Avoid museum/exhibition language.
- Use "clue" and "evidence" instead of "exhibit" in player-facing text.
